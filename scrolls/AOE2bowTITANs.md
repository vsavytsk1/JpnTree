# Age of Empires II: A Deep Reference on Design, Mechanics, Balance, and Technical Internals

## TL;DR
- **AoE2's enduring design is built on Bruce Shelley's Sid-Meier-derived philosophy of "a series of interesting decisions" and an "inverted pyramid of decision making,"** expressed mechanically through four resources, a four-age progression gate, a deep prerequisite-driven tech tree, and a "soft" counter system where every unit interaction depends on numbers, upgrades, terrain, and micro — an interdependency that keeps the 1999 game competitively fresh in its 2019 Definitive Edition.
- **Asymmetric balance across ~53 civilizations is achieved by combining shared baselines (the common tech tree) with deliberate subtractions (missing techs) and additions (civ/team bonuses, unique units, two unique techs), continuously tuned via data-driven patches informed by community win/pick-rate statistics** (aoestats/aoe2insights) — with developers targeting rough parity while accepting that some roles (notably pure infantry) remain contested.
- **Technically, AoE2 runs on Ensemble's data-driven Genie Engine: a 2D-sprite, isometric, deterministic-lockstep RTS whose units, techs, and civ bonuses live in editable .dat data files, with AI in Lisp-like .per scripts** — an architecture now documented and reimplemented by the open-source openage project (C++/Python, nyan data language).

## Key Findings

1. **Design philosophy is explicit and traceable.** Bruce Shelley, co-designer, repeatedly credited Sid Meier's principles: "the player should have the fun, not the designer, programmer, or computer," "great game-play is a stream of interesting decisions the player must resolve," and the "inverted pyramid of decision making" (few decisions early, multiplying as the game absorbs the player). The four resources exist specifically to widen the decision tree relative to two-resource RTS games.

2. **The four ages are both a pacing device and a dependency gate.** Advancing requires two buildings of the current age plus a resource payment (Feudal 500 food; Castle 800 food + 200 gold; Imperial 1000 food + 800 gold) and fixed research times (Feudal 130s, Castle 160s, Imperial 190s). Each age unlocks tiers of units, buildings, and techs, and civ bonuses often scale by age.

3. **The counter system is "soft," not pure rock-paper-scissors.** It runs on armor classes and bonus damage. The base triangle (cavalry > archers > infantry > cavalry) is heavily modified by exceptions (Skirmishers vs archers, Camels/Spearmen vs cavalry, Eagles/Huskarls vs archers), and by "counter-flipping" and "finicky" scaling where army size, upgrades, terrain, and micro flip outcomes.

4. **Unique units and unique technologies are civ-defining power spikes**, gated behind the Castle (650 stone, 200s build). Since The Forgotten, each civ has two unique techs (one Castle Age, one Imperial). The Castle is thus simultaneously a fortress, a unique-unit factory, a Trebuchet source, and the Imperial Age gate.

5. **The economy is the strategic center of gravity.** Four resources, distinct gather rates per source, dropoff logistics, finite farms (renewable via rebuild), and a long chain of economic upgrades create the eco-vs-military tension ("boom vs. pressure") that structures all high-level play.

6. **Micro vs. macro is the core skill axis.** Macro (constant villager/unit production, teching, map control) vs. micro (kiting, mangonel splitting, monk conversion timing, hit-and-run). APM matters but efficient decision-priority matters more.

7. **Technically the game is transparent and moddable by design.** Data-driven .dat files, a Lisp-like .per AI language, a scenario editor with triggers (and now XS scripting), and a documented reverse-engineering/openage ecosystem.

## Details

### 1. Design philosophy & developer intent

**Bruce Shelley's principles.** Shelley — who assisted Sid Meier on *Civilization* and *Railroad Tycoon* before co-founding Ensemble Studios in 1995 — is the clearest voice on AoE's design intent. In interviews and his widely-cited essay "Guidelines for Developing Successful Games" (Game Developer/Gamasutra), he articulates principles inherited from Meier: "The player should have the fun, not the designer, programmer, or computer"; "great game-play is a stream of interesting decisions the player must resolve"; and "the inverted pyramid of decision making (have few decisions to deal with first, and then let them multiply until the player is totally engrossed)." He also stresses "an interesting starting situation," "minimal barriers to entry," and "put the player in his dreams, where he or she is the hero." His summary design ethos: "make a game of very high quality" and "design for your audience, not yourself; make commercial art, not fine art."

Shelley's own account of what he lobbied for in the original game: "I lobbied for randomly generated maps, levels of difficulty, non-cheating AI, an interesting economy, multiple victory conditions." He also described the Ensemble method inherited from board gaming and Meier: "prototype early, play every day, design by playing, fixing, removing, and adding."

**Why four ages and four resources.** The four-age structure was a deliberate scope decision. Shelley: "We thought the whole gamut of history was too much to encompass... if we can make ancient history work, there are future periods we could move into with follow-up games. We were building a series in our mind." He also framed the tech tree as a differentiation engine: "That becomes part of your tech tree, and it gave us more hooks: more items to work with later, more changes in the database later. That created more differentiation in terms of statistics and attributes." On process, the tech tree was iterated in both directions: they "started with the technologies and tried to fit the game to them but then reversed ourselves and decided what effect we needed for the good of the game." The four resources (food, wood, gold, stone) — versus the two typical of *StarCraft*-style RTS — were chosen to expand the decision tree and slow players into more deliberate city-building.

**Sandy Petersen.** Petersen, who came to Ensemble from id Software (Doom/Quake) in June 1997, was lead designer of *The Rise of Rome* (AoE1 expansion) and *The Conquerors* (AoE2 expansion), and "one of the top designers" on AoE2 base game. He designed the four new civs per expansion, created the Aztec/Maya building sets, and deliberately gave the Mayans an instant economic bonus "so a first-time player could IMMEDIATELY see that the Mayans were cool." He also championed restraint in unit design: on The Conquerors, "We only added one new unit — Petards... Adding tons of little picky units with niggling differences isn't my way." (Note: some of Petersen's self-reported development claims have been publicly disputed, though his lead-designer credits on the expansions are independently documented.)

**Definitive Edition intent (Forgotten Empires / World's Edge / Microsoft).** Forgotten Empires — a team of former modders — stated the DE project goal shifted from the HD Edition's "let's bring Age of Empires back to the players" to "we need to make the best Age II possible." Lead developer Bert Beeckman emphasized preserving unit readability ("One of the beauties of Age of Empires II is that you see the units and you know what it is") and keeping the game 2D-sprite-based despite 3D-source art, because instant unit recognition is "very, very important in RTS games." Every asset was remade from scratch (originals were lost) while matching the 1999 silhouettes. On new civs, they weighed "gameplay versus history," acknowledging "Age of Empires has always been a bit of a Hollywood version of history."

### 2. The age progression system

Four ages span a ~1,000-year timeframe: **Dark Age** → **Feudal Age** (High Middle Ages onset) → **Castle Age** → **Imperial Age** (Renaissance). Advancing requires (a) constructing two buildings of the current age (with exceptions — Khmer need no buildings; Persians/Malay/Italians get discounts/speedups) and (b) paying resources while a Town Center research timer runs:

- **Feudal:** 500 food, 130 seconds. Unlocks Blacksmith, Market, Archery Range, Stable, Barracks upgrades, watch towers, palisade→stone transition, and Feudal military (Man-at-Arms, Scout Cavalry, Archers, Skirmishers, Spearmen).
- **Castle:** 800 food + 200 gold, 160 seconds. Requires two Feudal buildings. Unlocks the Castle, Monastery, University, Siege Workshop, second Town Centers, Knights, Crossbows, Pikemen, Mangonels, Monks, unique units, and Castle-Age unique techs.
- **Imperial:** 1000 food + 800 gold, 190 seconds. Requires a Castle or two Castle-Age buildings. Unlocks gunpowder (for most civs), heavy siege (Siege Onager, Bombard Cannon), Trebuchets from the start, Wonders, final unit upgrades (Paladin, Champion, Arbalester, Halberdier), and Imperial unique techs.

Civ-specific age modifiers are numerous and strategically load-bearing (all detailed in the Fandom Civilization page footnotes): Malay advance to each age 66% faster (e.g., Feudal in 78s instead of 130s, Castle in 96s instead of 160s); Byzantines advance to Imperial 33% cheaper (667 food + 533 gold instead of 1000 + 800); Italians age up 15% cheaper at every step; Burgundians unlock economic techs an age early; Bengalis/Cumans/Bohemians and others get age-transition bonuses (e.g., Bengalis spawn 2 villagers per Town Center on reaching Feudal). The age system functions as the master dependency gate: the tech tree is layered so that access to any advanced unit or tech is transitively gated by age plus building plus (sometimes) prerequisite tech.

### 3. Civilizations & balance

**How civs are differentiated.** The roster totals roughly **53 civilizations** per the Age of Empires Series Wiki: the base game (*The Age of Kings*) shipped with 13, all included in DE, and the game has grown through successive expansions — most recently *The Last Chieftains* (Feb 17, 2026) adding the Mapuche, Muisca, and Tupi. (Steam's base package lists 42; official marketing cites "more than 45" ranked civs.) Each civ is defined by: (1) a set of **civilization bonuses** (passive, often age-scaling — e.g., Franks' Foragers +15% and cheaper castles, Britons' +1/+2 archer range in Castle/Imperial); (2) a **team bonus** benefiting allies (e.g., Britons' faster-working Archery Ranges); (3) **one to three unique units** trained at the Castle (or a secondary building like the Bulgarian Krepost or Sicilian Donjon); (4) **two unique technologies** (Castle + Imperial Age); and critically (5) **a deliberately pruned tech tree** — the units and upgrades the civ *cannot* access.

The missing-tech design is central to balance: Britons lack Bloodlines and Thumb Ring; Franks lack most of the archer tree but get free Paladin-line upgrades; Goths lack much defensive tech but get cheap, fast-spammable infantry; Byzantines are the deliberate "all-rounder" with a near-full tree but few economic bonuses. Players are advised to read missing techs as a signal of what a civ is *not* meant to do.

**Balance philosophy.** Developers tune asymmetric civs toward rough parity using community data — sites like aoestats.io and aoe2insights aggregate ranked ladder win rates and pick rates by civ, map, and Elo. Live-patch reasoning is published: for example, a monk-conversion rework introduced the Castle-Age **Devotion** tech and re-costed **Faith**, with the stated aim "to reduce the frustrating elements of conversions while keeping the relative power level the same." Balance is contested but tight: on the 1v1 Random Map ladder (ALL rating, ~1.03 million games), aoestats lists the top five civs as **Romans (54.14%), Bulgarians (53.41%), Vikings (52.46%), Malay (52.16%), and Wu (52.12%)**, with **Britons at 53.41%** (64,047 picks). At the other end, infantry-oriented civs sit near the bottom: **Burmese 46.98%** (20,031 picks) and **Bohemians 47.22%** (40,531 picks). The community reads this ~7-point spread as evidence the game is well-balanced but not perfectly so. A long-running critique holds that the intended rock-paper-scissors is really "paper-paper-stone," because infantry (especially the Militia line) underperforms its supposed role; developers have repeatedly buffed infantry (Supplies tech, cheaper Longswords, faster attack) with limited effect on pick rates — and the two lowest-win-rate civs above are both classic infantry civs.

**Civ identity archetypes.** Community and developer framing sorts civs into eco/boom-oriented (Mayans, Chinese, Franks), aggression/military (Huns, Mongols, Aztecs), and defensive/turtle (Teutons, Koreans, Byzantines, Turks for gunpowder) — with "power spikes" tied to when a civ's bonuses and unique tech peak (e.g., Franks in Castle-Age Knights; Britons in Imperial-Age Arbalest; Goths in the late Imperial infantry flood after Perfusion).

**Unique units & unique techs mechanics.** Castle unique units share a structural weakness — they require the 650-stone, 200-second Castle to produce, making early mass difficult (mitigated by Goths' Anarchy → Huskarls at Barracks, Huns' Marauders → Tarkans). Since The African Kingdoms, civs with a second unique unit train it at a non-Castle building, granting instant Castle-Age access. Unique techs range from mild to transformative (e.g., Britons' Warwolf giving Trebuchets blast damage and 100% accuracy; Byzantines' Logistica giving Cataphracts trample damage; Goths' Perfusion for infantry production speed). Some unique techs are team-wide (Kasbah, Cuman Mercenaries, Butalmapu).

### 4. The tech tree as a dependency graph

The tech tree is a directed acyclic graph of prerequisites: **age → building → (sometimes) prerequisite tech → unit/upgrade**. Key branches:

- **Blacksmith (15 techs, 5 per age from Feudal):** attack lines — Forging→Iron Casting→Blast Furnace (+1/+1/+2 infantry & cavalry melee attack); Fletching→Bodkin Arrow→Bracer (+1 attack/+1 range each, to archers, cav archers, arrow-firing ships, towers, castles, TCs); armor lines — Scale/Chain/Plate Mail (infantry), Scale/Chain/Plate Barding (cavalry), Padded/Leather/Ring Archer Armor (archers). Community heuristic: for ranged units prioritize attack (Bracer especially, since it adds range); for melee prioritize armor; melee attack matters least because of pervasive bonus damage.
- **University:** Ballistics (projectile tracking of moving targets — considered near-mandatory), Masonry/Architecture (building HP/armor), Murder Holes, Chemistry (gunpowder prerequisite, +1 projectile damage), Bombard Tower, Fortified Wall, Guard/Keep tower upgrades, Siege Engineers, Treadmill Crane, Heated Shot, Arrowslits.
- **Economy:** Loom (Town Center, Dark Age — +HP/armor to villagers); Wheelbarrow→Hand Cart (villager speed + carry capacity); Double-Bit Axe→Bow Saw→Two-Man Saw (wood); Gold Mining→Gold Shaft Mining; Stone Mining→Stone Shaft Mining; Horse Collar→Heavy Plow→Crop Rotation (farm food); Lumber/Mining Camp techs.
- **Military production lines:** infantry Militia→Man-at-Arms→Long Swordsman→Two-Handed Swordsman→Champion; archer Archer→Crossbowman→Arbalester; cavalry Scout→Light Cavalry→Hussar and Knight→Cavalier→Paladin; each gated by age and building.

**Economic-upgrade payoff analysis.** Analyst Spirit of the Law frames upgrades by "payoff time" — how long an upgrade takes to recoup its resource cost — with a "5-minute payoff" as a benchmark for efficient booming investment. Farm-upgrade value (Horse Collar/Heavy Plow/Crop Rotation) comes primarily from delaying farm re-seeding (saving 60 wood per farm not rebuilt), with the benefit "noticed" after ~10, ~12, and ~15 minutes respectively — so Horse Collar/Heavy Plow are prioritized early and Crop Rotation is often deprioritized because players "float" extra wood by then.

### 5. Resource & economy systems

Four resources: **food** (sheep, boar, deer, berries, farms, fish, hunt), **wood** (trees only), **gold** (mines, relics, trade), **stone** (mines only). Gather rates differ by source and villager task; base rates cluster around 0.3–0.45/second before upgrades and walking losses. Notable specifics: farms are capped by the farm's own work rate (~0.4/s) below the villager's collect rate, yielding a practical maximum around 24 food/minute (higher with Slavs/Aztecs bonuses); gold and stone mining upgrade in two steps each; villagers deposit at dropoff buildings (Mill, Lumber Camp, Mining Camp, Town Center).

The **villager count** is the master eco metric: common guidance is to produce villagers to ~50% of the 200 population cap (100 vills), pushing to ~120–130 in dedicated booms. **Idle Town Center / idle villager time** is the key inefficiency to minimize. The central tension is **booming** (adding Town Centers and villagers for a long-term economic lead) versus **military pressure** (spending on army to deny the opponent's economy) — the strategic fulcrum of every game.

### 6. Micro vs. macro / APM

**Macro** = economic management: constant villager production, uninterrupted military production from multiple buildings, timely teching, farm re-seeding, and map control. **Micro** = individual-unit control in engagements. Documented techniques:

- **Archer/crossbow micro:** hit-and-run (attack, retreat before the enemy closes), and "stop micro" — pressing Stop so every in-range unit re-targets the nearest enemy, spreading arrows efficiently and dodging a chasing mangonel; patrol-attack for massed volleys; targeting weak units in small fights.
- **Mangonel micro:** aiming for maximum enemy hits while avoiding friendly fire (blast damage harms allies); in archer wars "the primary counter to a Mangonel is another Mangonel," and well-microed archers "split" using flank formation hotkeys to minimize mangonel value. A quirk: a **deleted mangonel's** final projectile deals full 100% blast damage with no distance/elevation falloff.
- **Monk micro:** conversion is probabilistic per 1.2-second interval — per aoe2database.com, "the time is divided in intervals of 1.2 seconds. In each interval, the Monk will have a chance to convert the unit, and that chance increases over time." A minimum time must elapse before any chance, and "when the maximum time is reached, the conversion is guaranteed" (its classic table shows the 12th interval at 14.60s = 100% for a normal unit; note the post-DE-rework Fandom lists a normal unit's base window as roughly interval 4 to 10, extended to 6–14 with Faith). Scout and Eagle lines have innate conversion resistance (Fandom: "conversion resistance level 8" versus 0 for standard units, with longer 8-to-10 intervals) plus a monk-bonus attack, making them the premier monk counters. Managing faith regeneration and target selection (converting the most valuable enemy unit) is the skill.
- **Ballistics dodging:** because projectiles lead the target's position, sharp direction changes can make even Ballistics-enabled shots miss.

APM enables these but the community consensus (and Spirit-of-the-Law-style analysis) is that *decision priority* — knowing what to do next and never idling production — beats raw speed at most levels.

### 7. Unique technologies / power spikes

Unique techs are the clearest "power spike" levers, researched at the Castle. Examples of transformative effects: **Warwolf** (Britons, Trebuchet blast + 100% accuracy), **Logistica** (Byzantines, Cataphract trample + anti-infantry), **Stirrups** (Bulgarians, +33% cavalry attack speed), **Bagains** (Bulgarian Militia-line +5 armor), **Bearded Axe** (Franks, +1 Throwing Axeman range), **El Dorado/Garland Wars** (Aztec/Maya combat boosts), **Perfusion** (Goth infantry production). Combined with reaching a new age (which unlocks final unit upgrades), unique techs create discrete moments where a civ's effective strength jumps — the strategic reason timing attacks around your own power spike (and the enemy's weakness window) is decisive.

### 8. Strategy: build orders, openings, counters

**Build orders** are optimized opening action sequences measured in population and villager assignments. Canonical openings:

- **Scout rush (~21–22 pop):** wood+food economy feeding Feudal Scout Cavalry for early raiding; best on civs with Bloodlines. Pros use tighter 18–19 pop versions; 22 pop is the safer beginner standard.
- **Drush (Dark Age Militia rush):** 2–3 Militia to harass/delay, often flowing into Fast Castle or archers.
- **Archer rush / Man-at-Arms rush:** Feudal aggression via ranged or upgraded infantry.
- **Fast Castle (~28–30 pop, or 22+2 for booming civs):** minimal Feudal commitment to reach Castle Age fast for Knights or a 3-TC boom. Pros hit Feudal around 10:10 and Castle around 16–20 minutes with clean execution.
- **Tower rush (Trush):** forward towers to deny enemy resources.

**Scouting** drives everything — build-order choice cascades from what the opponent is doing. The counter system (armor classes + bonus damage) means strategic decisions chain: seeing archers → add Skirmishers/cavalry; seeing Knights → add Pikemen/Camels/Monks; seeing siege → add cavalry/Eagles. In the Imperial "trash war" (gold depleted), armies converge on Halberdiers + Hussars + Elite Skirmishers + siege, and the counter advantage among trash units decides late games.

### 9. Technical / code / engine details

**The Genie Engine.** AoE1, AoE2, and *Star Wars: Galactic Battlegrounds* run on Ensemble's Genie Engine — a 2D, isometric-tileset, sprite-based RTS engine. Though DE has 4K sprites and modern rendering, it remains fundamentally 2D-sprite (the 3D look is an illusion; art is 3D-rendered then flattened to sprites). Networking is **deterministic lockstep**: clients exchange commands and simulate identically (the Bettner networking postmortem is the canonical reference), which is why desyncs ("out of sync") are a known failure mode and why recorded games are compact command logs.

**Data-driven design.** Units, buildings, technologies, civ bonuses, and effects are defined in the **empires2_x2_p1.dat** data file (and DRS archives for graphics/sound), not hardcoded. The community **Advanced Genie Editor (AGE)**, shipped in DE's Tools_Builds directory, edits these .dat parameters directly — unit stats, armor/attack classes, tech effect chains, civ bonuses. Techs are modeled as "effect commands" that patch attributes (e.g., "+1 attack to armor class X for units of civ Y"). This is why civ bonuses and upgrades are, mechanically, data patches rather than code.

**Combat resolution formula.** AoE2 uses an additive attack/armor system. The core formula (community "Combat Basics" guide, corroborated by the Fandom wiki) is **(attack − armor) + (bonus damage − bonus-damage resistance) = final damage**, where each bracket is floored at 0 per class and the **final result is floored at a minimum of 1** (0.1 for buildings in AoE1). The Fandom "Attack" page states it directly: "The minimum damage is set to 1... if the damage dealt is less than that value." Bonus damage is applied against hidden armor classes and is **not** reduced by melee/pierce armor — the reason a fully-upgraded Halberdier deals ~32+ to a Paladin regardless of its armor (worked example: 10 attack − 5 armor + 32 bonus = 37; even at 12 armor the base is nullified to 0 but 32 bonus still lands). Multipliers layer on: elevation (~1.25 attacking downhill, ~0.75 uphill), 0.5 for mishit projectiles, and blast-radius falloff. Hit points are tracked as fractional floats; the UI shows only the integer part, and a unit dies when HP drops below 1.

**Ballistics & projectiles.** Without Ballistics, ranged units fire at the target's *current* position, missing moving targets; Ballistics makes projectiles lead the target. It's a per-projectile property (ID-editable), which is why converted units inherit the new owner's Ballistics status and units sharing a projectile share the effect.

**AI scripting (.per files).** The classic AI uses a Lisp-like rule language: rules take the form `(defrule (facts) => (actions))`. If all facts are true, all actions fire. Scripts use "strategic numbers" to shape behavior (you can't directly command units, only mold tendencies), with limits (max 16 fact+action combination per rule; up to 999 rules). The reference document (CPSB) ships on the expansion CD. DE added **XS scripting** (a C-like language, also used in random map scripts and scenario triggers via `xs-script-call`). DE also introduced a new, stronger default AI that "easily defeated" the older HD AI in testing, plus improved pathfinding and quality-of-life (shift-queued villager tasks, farm auto-reseed).

**Scenario editor & triggers.** The Genie scenario editor exposes a trigger system (conditions → effects) for custom scenarios and campaigns; DE extended it with XS scripting. Random maps are defined by RMS scripts.

**openage (open-source reimplementation).** The volunteer **openage** project (SFTtech, C++20 + Python3, Qt, OpenGL, SDL2) is a from-scratch Genie reimplementation that converts original DE assets. Its most illuminating architectural choice for understanding the engine is the **nyan** data language: everything (units, techs, civ bonuses) is a plain-text object with inheritance, and upgrades/research are modeled as **patches** to objects. This clarifies a Genie quirk: the original engine upgrades a unit by *replacing* the object with a higher-tier one (causing the known "converted units don't get later upgrades" behavior), whereas nyan patches attributes on-the-fly. openage documents that "the behavior of the original game is mostly undocumented," so its specs are reverse-engineered approximations. As of its recent releases openage is a tech demo (gameplay "basically non-functional" during an architecture rewrite), using flow-field pathfinding and an event-driven, component-based (ECS) simulation.

### 10. The "interesting decisions" & interdependency theme

AoE2's defining quality is that every system feeds every other, producing cascading decisions:

- **Eco ↔ military:** every villager not made is army now; every soldier made is eco later. The age-up payment (food + gold) directly trades against villager/military production timing.
- **Age ↔ tech ↔ unit:** you cannot make Crossbows without Castle Age + Archery Range + the Crossbow upgrade; the Castle simultaneously gates Imperial Age, unique units, and Trebuchets — so one 650-stone building decision ripples across defense, offense, and progression.
- **Counter cascades:** scouting an opponent's unit choice forces your counter, which they counter, which reshapes both economies (trash vs. gold units) and both tech paths.
- **Micro ↔ macro tension:** time spent microing a fight is time not spent producing or teching — the deliberate opportunity cost that Shelley's "stream of interesting decisions" is built on.
- **Civ design as constraint web:** a missing tech (e.g., no Bloodlines) redirects a civ away from one unit and toward its bonuses, so civ identity emerges from the interaction of additions and subtractions rather than any single bonus.

The community analyst consensus (e.g., the "weirdest game of rock-paper-scissors" analysis) is that this interdependency — "counter-flipping" and "finicky" matchups where the answer to "is this unit good?" is always "it depends" — is precisely what has kept the game strategically unsolved and competitively alive for over two decades.

## Recommendations

**For a reader seeking to understand the game deeply, prioritize sources in this order:**
1. **Primary developer material** — Bruce Shelley's "Guidelines for Developing Successful Games" and his interviews (Game Developer/Gamasutra, Arcade Attack, Shacknews oral history); Matt Pritchard's AoK postmortem (Gamasutra, 2000); the Beeckman DE interview. These establish *intent*.
2. **Mechanics references** — the Age of Empires Fandom wiki (aoe.fandom.com) for stats/formulas, aoe2techtree.net for the dependency graph, and Spirit of the Law's analytical videos for cost-effectiveness/payoff reasoning. Note clearly that wiki stats are community-data-mined (via AGE) rather than officially published.
3. **Balance data** — aoestats.io / aoe2insights for win/pick rates, read alongside official patch notes for developer *reasoning*.
4. **Engine internals** — the openage docs and blog (blog.openage.dev) and the reverse-engineering handbook for engine architecture; AGE and the .per/XS scripting guides for the data/AI layer.

**Staged learning/analysis path:** (a) master the four-resource economy and a single build order to internalize eco-military tension; (b) learn the counter system as armor-class + bonus-damage math, not memorized triangles; (c) study tech-tree dependencies and one civ's identity (additions + subtractions); (d) only then dig into engine/data internals.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:** if the game receives a major balance overhaul (e.g., an infantry rework that moves infantry-civ win rates like Burmese's 46.98% and Bohemians' 47.22% up toward ~50% consistently), re-evaluate the "paper-paper-stone" critique; if openage reaches playable gameplay parity, it becomes a primary rather than documentary source for engine behavior; if Microsoft/World's Edge publishes official internal design documents (some Shelley papers are archived at The Strong museum), those should supersede community reverse-engineering.

## Caveats

- **Official vs. community-derived knowledge.** Almost all precise numbers (gather rates, conversion intervals, damage multipliers, age costs) are community-data-mined via the Advanced Genie Editor or reverse-engineered by openage, not officially published. They are highly reliable through cross-checking but are not vendor documentation. openage itself states the original engine's behavior "is mostly undocumented."
- **Original vs. Definitive Edition differences.** Many mechanics, civ bonuses, and balance values have changed across AoK (1999), The Conquerors (2000), HD (2013), and DE (2019) plus DE's many patches and expansions (Lords of the West, Dawn of the Dukes, Dynasties of India, Return of Rome, The Mountain Royals, Chronicles, the 2025 Three Kingdoms content, and The Last Chieftains in 2026). Numbers cited here reflect DE's recent state where possible; specific values shift patch-to-patch, including the monk-conversion figures (the pre- and post-DE-rework interval systems differ).
- **Contested balance opinions.** The "infantry is underpowered / paper-paper-stone" thesis and civ tier lists are community opinion, not settled fact; developers and pros disagree on degree. Win-rate spreads are real but modest (roughly 47–54% across the ladder).
- **Sandy Petersen's self-reported claims** about his contributions carry a documented dispute (primarily regarding his Doom-era work); his AoE expansion lead-designer credits are independently verified, but some specifics rest on his own recollection.
- **Speculative/forward-looking items** (e.g., openage reaching full playability, future balance patches) are explicitly uncertain and framed as such.
- Where community sources conflicted on exact figures (older AoK vs. current DE gather rates and conversion windows), the DE-era value is used and the discrepancy flagged.
