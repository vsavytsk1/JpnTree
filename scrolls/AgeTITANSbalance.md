# The "Nonsensical IF Conditions" and Balance Scaffolding of Age of Empires II

## TL;DR
- **AoE2's counter system is not code — it is data.** Nearly every "weird" behavior (bonus damage, the counter triangle, civ identity, the minimum-damage floor, elevation, conversion resistance) is implemented as hidden unit attributes and additive/multiplicative "effect commands" that patch base stats, stored in the `empires2*.dat` file and editable in the Advanced Genie Editor. The real counter engine is a system of ~30+ hidden **armor classes** where a bonus reads "IF target is in armor class X, add N damage," and that bonus is NOT reduced by the target's melee/pierce armor.
- **The special-case rules are real and precise:** a hard minimum of 1 damage per hit (0.1 for buildings in AoE1); a fixed ±25% elevation multiplier (1.25× from high ground / 0.75× from low — an AoE2 value, whereas the 1.5×/0.67× figures belong to *Age of Empires I*); the Ballistics projectile-lead system with a 0.5× damage penalty for stray hits; the deleted-Mangonel full-blast trick; probabilistic 1.2-second conversion rolls with hardcoded resistance for scouts/eagles/elephants; and per-unit blast-attack/blast-defense levels that decide friendly fire.
- **Civilizations are defined as much by missing techs as by bonuses,** and a handful break the normal rules with genuinely hardcoded exceptions (Goths' Anarchy Huskarl-at-Barracks and free Loom; Khmer needing no prerequisite buildings; Sicilians' bonus-damage-resistance flag; scout/eagle/elephant conversion resistance; Lithuanian relic attack; Konnik/Ratha unit-swaps). The openage reverse-engineering project shows why these appear "hardcoded": the classic Genie Engine replaces the *whole unit object* on upgrade, so quirks like converted units never getting later upgrades fall out of the data model itself.

## Key Findings

**1. The damage formula and its floors.** AoE2 uses an additive attack/armor system. For each of a unit's attack types, the game subtracts the target's corresponding armor; positive results are summed, then bonus damage (again armor-class-subtracted) is added, then the elevation multiplier is applied. The result is floored: per the Age of Empires Series Wiki's "Attack" page, *"The minimum damage is set to 1 (0.1 for buildings in Age of Empires), if the damage dealt is less than that value... The unit dies when the remaining hit points reach below 1 (does not have to be less than or equal to 0)."* Fractional HP is tracked internally though only integers display. Crucially, the 1-damage floor can be satisfied by *either* the base attack or bonus damage — a fully-upgraded Halberdier vs Paladin does (10−12 = 0 clamped) + (32 bonus − 0) = 32, the game allowing base damage to be fully nullified because bonus damage already exceeds the floor.

**2. Armor classes are the counter engine.** Every unit carries a list of attack classes and a list of armor classes ("damage classes"). Base Melee (class 4), Base Pierce (class 3) and Leitis/anti-Leitis (class 31) are on every unit. Bonus damage exists because a unit has a positive attack value against a special class (e.g., Cavalry = class 8) and the target carries that class with armor value 0. If the target lacks the class, its armor is treated as 10,000, nullifying that attack. A non-zero armor value in a class = bonus-damage *resistance*: the Cataphract has +16 Cavalry armor, so Halberdiers deal only +16 instead of +32; the Elephant Archer has −4 cavalry-archer armor, taking *extra* bonus damage. This is why the counter triangle has counterintuitive holes: Camel Riders are NOT in the Cavalry class (they have their own "Camel Units" class taking less anti-cavalry bonus), cavalry archers are a separate class from foot archers, Eagles are "Shock Infantry" (Champions get a bonus vs that class), and every unique unit carries the "Unique Unit" class (class 19) that the Samurai's anti-unique-unit bonus keys off. Buildings sit in multiple building classes at once ("All Buildings"/class 11 vs "Standard Buildings") specifically so Masonry/Architecture armor doesn't cancel infantry's anti-building bonus.

**3. Special-case combat conditionals** — elevation, ballistics, blast/friendly-fire, the delete trick, conversion, snare — are documented in Details; each is a genuine hardcoded or data-flagged exception a re-implementer must model exactly.

**4. Balance philosophy** targets rough parity across deliberately asymmetric civs, informed by ladder win/pick-rate data, with a long-running unresolved debate that infantry is underpowered ("paper-paper-stone").

**5. The engine/data model** explains why the exceptions exist as data: the Genie Engine replaces unit objects wholesale on upgrade; openage's nyan model replaces this with reversible "patches."

## Details

### A. Special-case / "nonsensical" combat conditionals

**Minimum damage floor.** Every attack deals ≥1 (buildings ≥0.1 in AoE1). The floor is applied to the *sum* of (attack−armor) + (bonus−bonus-resist), never below 1. A community test (Steam "Combat Basics: How Damage is Calculated") demonstrated the floor is on the total, not added atop bonus: *"the Mangudai dealt 4 damage per shot, but when I increased its attack to 200, the damage increased to 9 per shot. If the minimum 1 damage were being added to the bonus damage, the Mangudai should be doing 5 damage per shot instead of 4"* (Elite Mangudai vs a Siege Ram with 195 pierce armor).

**Elevation.** AoE2's multiplier is a *fixed* ±25% regardless of how many elevation levels separate attacker and target. Per the AoE Series Wiki "Elevation" page, *"units and buildings gain a +25% attack multiplier when attacking downhill... and a -25% attack penalty when attacking uphill... This leads to units on higher ground having a 66.66% advantage over lower-ground enemies; a unit on lower ground will be 40% worse."* The stored damage-formula term is k_elev = 1.25 downhill / 0.75 uphill / 1 otherwise, applied to both melee and ranged. The common "1.5×/0.67×" figures belong to *Age of Empires I*, not AoE2. Hills grant BOTH an offensive +25% and a defensive −25%; **cliffs grant only the offensive bonus, not the defensive reduction.** Because HP displays as integers, the engine varies individual shot damage to average ~25% — a community test observed a tower on a hill dealing *"the same repeating sequence: 17, 16, 16, 16, 17.... the game tries to get as close to 25% as it can."* DE update 141935 extended elevation levels from the original 1–7 up to 1–16. Civ modifiers exist (Tatars +50% instead of +25% from high ground; Georgians' buildings take −40% instead of −25%).

**The deleted-Mangonel / Onager full-blast trick.** A Mangonel's blast normally tapers with distance from the target point, and a shot fired from lower elevation is reduced to 75%. But a projectile's blast distribution and accuracy are read from the *firing unit*; when the unit is deleted (or dies) mid-flight, the dead unit "has no accuracy, so it defaults to 100%," AND the distance taper and elevation reduction do not apply — the final projectile deals full 100% damage to every unit in the blast radius. This is the mechanical basis of the "delete your own Mangonels" micro. (Warwolf Trebuchets are given 100% accuracy precisely so their blast reliably damages all units.) DE update 153015 made secondary Mangonel projectiles deal minimum damage individually even on attack-ground.

**Blast damage and friendly fire — the blast-attack/blast-defense level IF check.** Every unit has a Blast Attack Level and every owned unit a Blast Defense Level. The rule: **a unit takes blast from an attacker whose Blast Attack Level is equal or lower, and only inside the radius.** All player-owned units have blast defense 2. Mangonels/Onagers have blast attack 2 → they CAN damage your own units (2 ≤ 2). Scorpions have blast attack 3 → they CANNOT hit friendlies (they still pass-through-damage enemies in a line, at 50% to non-primary targets). Onagers have blast attack 1 → they can even cut trees (trees have blast defense 1), which Mangonels (2) cannot. Blast Attack flag 4 explicitly disables friendly fire; this is why Demolition Ships, despite huge AoE, do NOT harm friendly/allied units in DE (per the wiki, *"Heavy Demolition Ships explode when killed, dealing blast damage to any nearby units. However, they do not cause friendly fire damage, so any nearby allied units will not be damaged"*). Petards, by contrast, DO friendly-fire in their tiny 0.5-tile blast (confirmed by the Jurchens' −50% friendly-fire civ bonus applying to them). Common friendly-fire units: Mangonel line, Bombard Cannon, Cannon Galleon, Dromon, Turtle Ship, Warwolf Trebuchet. DE update 87863 removed friendly fire from Greek-Fire Bombard Towers. **Siege Rams have a hardcoded quirk:** per the "Area of Effect" page, *"Siege Rams when attacking buildings do not attack nearby cavalry units, even with their 4 melee attack. Nearby buildings are affected... This effect is also hardcoded."*

**Self-damage / recoil.** Units able to damage their own/allied units are exactly the friendly-fire blast units above (Mangonel line and Bombard Cannon are the classic offenders that catch your own melee/archers). Demolition Ships and Petards are suicide units — they self-destruct on attack, on death, or on manual Delete, always dealing full damage — but the demo-ship line's blast is enemy-only in DE, whereas Petards friendly-fire. Petards carry enormous class-specific bonuses (+900 vs Walls/Gates, +500 vs All Buildings, +100 vs Castles, +60 vs Siege) and are the only military unit with *no* armor class, so they take no bonus damage.

**Ballistics and the 0.5× stray-hit penalty.** Without Ballistics, ranged units fire at a target's *current* position, missing anything moving laterally. Ballistics is a flag on the *projectile* (value 1 = lead the target). A separate projectile flag (value 2) makes a stray projectile that hits a non-target deal full damage instead of the default 50%; value 3 = both. Because Ballistics affects the projectile, converted units get it based on the *new* owner's research, and units sharing a projectile are all-or-nothing. It does not affect melee-at-range units (Mameluke, Throwing Axeman, Gbeto, Chakram). Gunpowder can't get Ballistics; the Portuguese Arquebus is a partial substitute but is widely reported as barely functional on Bombard Cannons/Cannon Galleons because their projectile speed is treated as arrow-speed. DE update 44725 shrank projectile hit-zones, making crossbow fire meaningfully harder to dodge than in the old UserPatch era. DE update 125283 made Scorpion and Ballista Elephant projectiles obey Ballistics; Turtle Ships stopped being affected in update 141935.

**Town Center / tower arrow logic.** A TC fires arrows only if garrisoned. Each garrisoned villager adds ~2.5 DPS of "firepower"; crossing each multiple of the building's per-arrow firepower adds an arrow (villagers add ~1 arrow each in a TC up to a cap of ~10 counted; DE gives villagers 2.5 building-attack when garrisoned, boosted by Fletching/Chemistry/Svan Towers). Garrisoned archer units add arrows scaled by their own DPS (Hand Cannoneers ~2, unupgraded Plumed Archers ~0.5 pre-fix), but every extra arrow does the *building's* damage, not the unit's. TCs uniquely fire at adjacent units without needing Murder Holes but gain no range from Blacksmith upgrades. Non-mounted units heal while garrisoned; TCs cannot be converted. Loom interaction: a TC-garrison rush is deadly against an opponent who clicked to Feudal without Loom, since they can neither afford Loom nor keep villagers safe.

**Conversion as probabilistic IF checks.** Conversion runs in ~1.2-second "conversion intervals." A minimum number of intervals must pass before any chance exists; at the maximum, conversion is guaranteed. Historically (AoK/AoC and early DE) units used min 4 / max ~12 intervals with ~28% per-interval chance, and resistance was a "level" (scouts/light-cav high, buildings 3). The DE monk rework (Devotion introduced, Faith re-costed) explicitly aimed to "reduce the variability, creating more consistency." Per official Update 99311 notes: *"Monk conversion chance 25% ▶ 40%, interval 4-10 ▶ 5-9... With these changes, 80% of conversions will happen between 5 and 7 seconds."* Resistance is now expressed as added min/max seconds rather than a "level" for units (buildings still use the level). Per the "Conversion" page: *"With update 56005, Scout Cavalry and Eagle Scout lines do not have increased conversion resistance level, but instead take +4 minimum seconds (8 to 10) to convert. This property is hardcoded"* (later retuned toward +3 min/+1 max); and *"Bengalis: Elephants have conversion resistance level 2, and take +1 minimum and +2 maximum seconds to convert (5 to 12). This property is also hardcoded via some resource."* Faith adds +4/+4 (units 50% harder to convert); Devotion +1/+1 (15% harder); the Teutonic team bonus +3/+2; Sicilian First Crusade +4/+4 (identical to Faith). Multiple monks roll independently (chances don't simply add to 100%); all lose faith on a success unless Theocracy is researched. Switching targets doesn't reset the interval count (enabling near-instant "steal" conversions) — except building→unit switches now reset it (update 95810). Redemption is required to convert buildings and most siege (not Ballista Elephants or Petards). Converted units keep upgrades they had but never receive *future* upgrades.

**Snare (a negative finding).** The classic melee "snare" (attacked units slowed 35%) is an Age of Mythology / AoE3 mechanic, NOT a base AoE2 mechanic — AoE2's cavalry-beats-archer dynamic instead relies on the projectile-miss (Ballistics) system. A re-implementer must not add snare to model AoE2 faithfully; community discussion explicitly contrasts the two engines on this point.

### B. Per-civ hardcoded exceptions

- **Goths:** Loom researched instantly (free); **Anarchy** unique tech lets Huskarls be trained at the *Barracks* (normally a Castle-only unique unit) — a genuine rule-break; **Perfusion** = +100% Barracks work rate (stacks multiplicatively with their +20% team bonus); infantry cost −15/20/25/30% by age; +10 pop in Imperial. Balance deliberately keeps their early game mediocre so the flood only comes online late.
- **Sicilians:** land military units carry a **bonus-damage-resistance flag** (the "Bonus Damage Resist" attribute), multiplying all bonus damage — every class except Base Melee/Base Pierce/Leitis — by a fraction (0.5 at launch → 0.33 after update 66692, i.e. −50% then −33%). Serjeants build Donjons, get a free upgrade on aging up; First Crusade spawns Serjeants per TC and grants Faith-equivalent conversion resistance. Castles/farms build faster.
- **Khmer:** **no prerequisite buildings required to advance ages or unlock buildings** (a hardcoded exception to the tech-gating structure); farmers auto-deposit food with no drop-off (update 34699); villagers/Kings garrison in Houses (max 5); Scorpions +1 range; Ballista Elephants/Scorpions fire a half-strength extra bolt (Double Crossbow).
- **Cumans:** may build a **second Town Center in the Feudal Age** (normally Castle-Age-only) — a specifically enabled exception; also cheaper Feudal military buildings.
- **Lithuanians:** Knight-line and Leitis get **+1 attack per garrisoned Relic (max +4)**; the Leitis's attack *ignores melee armor* entirely (but not vs buildings/siege/ships), implemented via the dedicated Leitis/anti-Leitis class 31 that every unit carries. Relic-requiring techs were formerly hardcoded to tech IDs 699–703; DE de-hardcoded this so any tech with a "Relics Captured" cost works. (The relic bonus was nerfed from +5 to +4 in update 34055 because Lithuanian Paladins with 4 relics became the strongest unit in the game.)
- **Bengalis:** elephants resist conversion (hardcoded, above); spawn bonus villagers on aging up; Mahayana drops villager/monk pop to 0.9.
- **Konnik (Bulgarians):** dies into a Dismounted Konnik (infantry) — implemented by spawning a second unit on death; both forms count separately in the post-game score; a converted Konnik keeps the original owner's version even after dismounting.
- **Ratha (Dravidians):** switches melee/ranged mode via a whole-unit swap (the mechanic Ensemble originally planned for the Samurai).
- **Shrivamsha Rider (Gurjaras):** absorbs/dodges 5 (7 elite) projectiles over ~20s; cannot absorb projectiles aimed at allies.
- **Coustillier (Burgundians):** charge attack adds +20 (+25 elite) to its first blow, recharging over 40s; does not work on buildings; trained with charge full; the "charged attack" is a data-flagged ability now reused across Urumi/Fire Lancer/Turtle Ship/etc.
- **Others:** the Warrior Priest heals and carries relics (a returning unit), the Missionary is a mounted unconvertible monk, Flemish Revolution transforms all villagers into Flemish Militia irreversibly, and the Karambit Warrior costs 0.5 pop.

### C. Balance structure and philosophy

Civ bonuses, team bonuses and unique techs are all implemented as data "effect commands" (Set/Add/Multiply on a unit or tech attribute), not bespoke code — which is why a tech's stated effect and its actual data value can differ, and why bonuses can be surgically given to or withheld from specific civs. Since The Forgotten, every civ has two unique techs (one Castle-Age, one Imperial-Age); The Conquerors gave one each (Goths two). A civ's identity is defined heavily by *missing* techs — Britons lack Bloodlines and Thumb Ring; Franks lack most of the archer line but get a free Paladin upgrade; Byzantines are the deliberate all-rounder with a near-full tree.

The **infantry / "paper-paper-stone" debate** is the signature balance problem: the intended rock-paper-scissors (cavalry > archers > infantry > cavalry) collapses because the Militia line is too slow and too pop-inefficient to catch archers or trade with knights, so the community describes it as "paper-paper-stone" (infantry loses to both). Developers have tried Supplies (cheaper Militia-line food cost), faster/cheaper production, and the newer **Gambesons** tech (+1 pierce armor, granted only to civs that already have Supplies so it can be withheld from strong archer/cav civs), but community consensus is these have not fixed Castle-Age/early-Imperial infantry. The reason the buff is a *tech* rather than a base stat is explicitly so it can be given to some civs and not others (a free base buff would make Goths overpowered). Patches are informed by ladder statistics (aoestats.io, aoe2insights, aoe2.net win/pick-rate by elo and map), though top-bracket samples are tiny.

### D. Engine / data model (why these exceptions exist as data, not code)

The classic Genie Engine stores everything as attributes in `empires2*.dat` (a binary format with well-documented byte offsets), edited via the Advanced Genie Editor (AGE3), which in DE ships in `Tools_Builds` and is applied as a *mod* rather than by overwriting the base file. Technologies are modeled as "effect commands" that Set/Add/Multiply attributes (Resource Modifier, Attack/Armor class modifiers, Blast levels, etc.). A key structural quirk: **upgrades replace the whole unit object** (Militia→Man-at-Arms swaps every unit of the tier), which is why converted units can't receive later upgrades and why per-unit individual upgrades are impossible in AoE2.

The **openage** reverse-engineering project replaces this with the **nyan** data model, where objects are defined once and every change (research, upgrade, mod, civ bonus) is a reversible **patch** applied at runtime — no object replacement, so units can upgrade on the fly, individually, and conversion has no "replacement" bug. As openage's docs put it, *"in AoE2 it's all or nothing, upgrades always affect every unit of a tier... The replacement strategy leads to another problem: objects that are already deployed on the battlefield have to be replaced correctly. Converted units are known for having problems with that."* openage's converter reads the .dat into "GenieObjects" mirroring AGE, groups them into concepts (unit lines, age-ups, transform groups like trebuchets), then emits nyan. This confirms most "hardcoded" AoE2 behavior is actually data-flag driven; the genuinely hardcoded pieces (scout/eagle/elephant conversion resistance, siege-ram-ignores-cavalry blast) are the exceptions the wikis explicitly flag as "hardcoded." The `.per`/`.ai` script interpreter is a separate subsystem (deterministic lockstep simulation, RNG seeding, OOS checks) that the reverse-engineering and UserPatch work has mapped.

## Recommendations
- **To faithfully re-implement or model AoE2, build the armor-class system first — it is the backbone.** Model each unit as carrying two attribute lists (attack classes, armor classes); apply per-class subtraction, sum positives, add the armor-class-subtracted bonus, multiply by k_elev, then floor at 1 (0.1 for buildings). Treat "not in class" as armor 10,000.
- **Model the hidden flags explicitly:** Blast Attack Level / Blast Defense Level (the ≤ comparison for who takes blast), the projectile Ballistics flag (1/2/3), the Bonus Damage Resist multiplier (Sicilians), and per-unit min/max conversion-time modifiers. Do NOT model snare — it is not an AoE2 mechanic.
- **Treat the delete-Mangonel full-blast, cliff-has-no-defensive-bonus, and converted-units-miss-future-upgrades behaviors as required quirks, not bugs** — they are load-bearing for high-level play and re-implementation fidelity.
- **For balance work,** use aoestats.io / aoe2.net win-and-pick-rate data segmented by elo bracket and map, but weight top-bracket data cautiously (1650+ is only ~top 300 players, a tiny sample). A reasonable patch threshold: a civ persistently above ~52–53% or below ~47–48% win rate across a full patch cycle at a given bracket, corroborated by pick-rate skew — with single-patch numbers treated as provisional given monthly balance changes.
- **If designing new civs/units, prefer data-driven effects** (patched attributes, new armor classes, charge/dodge abilities) over engine edits; note the community critique that "charge attack" mechanics have become overused precisely because the Genie Engine makes truly novel mechanics hard to author.

## Caveats
- Many precise values (per-interval conversion %, exact garrison-arrow rounding, blast taper curves) come from community data-mining (AGE inspection, Spirit of the Law, AoEZone / Age of Kings Heaven testing) rather than official documentation, and some have shifted across DE patches — where I cite a value I have tried to date it to a patch/update number.
- Values differ across titles: AoK/AoC vs DE (monk rework and Devotion/Faith re-cost, projectile hit-zone change in update 44725, elevation levels 1–16, naval rework in update 169123) and vs *Age of Empires I* (elevation 1.5/0.67, 0.1 building floor, negative-armor bonus convention). Return of Rome introduced yet another reworked armor-class system.
- The targeted follow-up research produced a **correction to a common premise**: Demolition Ships do NOT friendly-fire in DE (their blast is enemy-only), and the correct AoE2 elevation multipliers are ±25% (1.25/0.75), not the 1.5/0.5 sometimes assumed — those are the Age of Empires I values.
- Win-rate figures fluctuate month to month with monthly balance patches; any single-patch civ number should be treated as provisional, and the very top elo bracket has too few games to be statistically reliable on its own.
